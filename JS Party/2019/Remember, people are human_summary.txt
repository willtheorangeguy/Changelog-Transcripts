• Benefits of higher-level comments (explaining motivation, context) vs. same-level comments (repeating code)
• Uses of in-line commenting for anomalies, workarounds, and temporary solutions
• Importance of comments in code for readability and understanding
• 80/20 rule: 20% of comments provide useful context, while 80% are apologies for poor coding practices
• Documentation as a reference vs. an introduction to the framework/language
• Contextual documentation that explains why certain patterns or libraries are used
• Use of templates in issue and PR creation to provide context and facilitate communication between developers
• Writing commit messages in the present tense for easier readability
• Prefixing commit messages with keywords like "fix", "chore", "feat", and "refactor"
• Using feedback ladders to organize and prioritize comments on PRs (e.g. "boulder", "mountain", "pebble", "sand")
• Considering the primary audience when giving or receiving feedback, focusing on empathy and treating others as humans with strengths, weaknesses, and goals
• Communicating with non-technical stakeholders, clients, and co-workers, taking into account their motivations and goals.
• Effective communication with non-technical stakeholders requires understanding their level of technical knowledge and context.
• It's essential to "speak at the right level of abstraction" when communicating with different individuals, avoiding both oversimplification and unnecessary explanation.
• Appealing to stakeholders' ego by making them feel more knowledgeable can be helpful in getting them to open up and share more about their needs and goals.
• Tools for bridging communication gaps include faster feedback loops, getting things in writing, active listening, paraphrasing, asking questions, and clarifying assumptions.
• Miscommunication often arises from unspoken or unclear expectations, and being able to refer back to previous discussions can help resolve issues.
• Setting clear scope and expectations for feedback
• Asking stakeholders to clarify what they want and focusing on specific functionality
• Presenting design concepts in low-fidelity formats to avoid distractions
• Avoiding technical jargon and assumptions about stakeholder understanding
• Establishing shared context by considering the other person's goals, incentives, and priorities
• Listening actively and asking questions to gauge understanding and context
• Technical support interactions can be improved by understanding the caller's level of technical expertise
• Asking about technical expertise allows support teams to tailor their communication and show that they value the customer's knowledge
• Active listening and attributing specific questions or feedback to individuals makes people feel heard and valued
• When communicating with multiple stakeholders, using their concerns against each other can help create a collaborative conversation
• Effective communication involves being relatable, maintaining a sense of reference, and framing problems in a way that resonates with the audience
• Communication styles may need to be adapted when interacting with users who are technical (e.g. dev rel)
• Finding ways to have users explain their problems and solutions in their own words
• Being relatable and avoiding sounding too technical or corporate
• Translating user-proposed solutions into actual problems that need solving
• Understanding the context behind a user's issue, rather than just focusing on the symptoms
• Reframing user communication to avoid making them feel stupid or uninformed
• Writing clear error messages to help users identify and solve their own issues
• Balancing technical expertise with communication skills when working with users
• Be upfront and transparent when communicating with users about potential solutions
• Empower users to think beyond the immediate problem they're facing
• Validate user feedback by acknowledging its potential impact on the project
• Ask users to show you how they're using the software or website to gain a deeper understanding of their experience
• Simulate real-life user experiences, such as testing on a mobile device in a distracting environment, to improve usability
• Don't assume that users will use your product the same way you do; be prepared for unexpected behaviors and challenges.
• Don't hate on or belittle users
• Treat users with compassion and empathy
• Recognize that users are human beings trying to accomplish real tasks
• Avoid dismissing user issues with "PEBKAC" (Problem Exists Between Keyboard And Chair) mentality
