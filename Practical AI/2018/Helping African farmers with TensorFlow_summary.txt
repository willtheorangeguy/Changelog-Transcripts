• Project introduction: using AI and machine learning to help farmers grow cassava better through a mobile app 
• Explanation of cassava as a root crop used for food, starch, flour, and other purposes
• Cassava is being grown as a crop for food and export, but it's affected by diseases such as cassava mosaic disease and brown streak disease
• These viral diseases can destroy leaves or roots of the plant, leading to reduced yields and no harvestable product
• The conventional method of diagnosing these diseases requires expert knowledge and is not feasible in many areas due to a shortage of skilled personnel
• AI-powered app aims to democratize access to technology by providing farmers with expertise through a model that can identify infected or healthy plants
• Collaboration between human experts and AI is necessary for developing accurate models, and AI is not meant to replace human farmers but augment their abilities
• Difficulty in correctly labeling plant disease data due to varying symptoms and co-infection
• Limited publicly available plant disease datasets, requiring the creation of a custom dataset for cassava
• Collaborative effort with IITA (International Institute for Tropical Agriculture) experts for accurate data collection
• Use of mobile devices for on-the-fly image capture and labeling in the field
• Development of a model to classify cassava diseases using the collected data
• Plans to share the model and weights openly, but uncertain about public availability of the dataset itself
• Mobile devices becoming the most ubiquitous platform for technology
• No Internet access in rural farming locations requiring app functionality offline
• Object detection used as a more effective alternative to classification for disease diagnosis due to performance and battery life issues with classification models
• Compatibility of app with various mobile devices, including Android 5.0 and above
• Development of metrics to evaluate app performance on different phones, prioritizing false negatives over false positives
• Model designed to be conservative in predictions, providing "I don't know" or "not confident enough" responses instead of incorrect diagnoses
• Mobile device constraints affected architecture and model decisions
• Transfer learning was necessary due to limited data for specific classes
• Mobile Net architecture performed well despite limitations
• Model was shrunk from Inception v4 without significant performance loss
• Collaboration with Google engineers provided helpful guidance and mentorship
