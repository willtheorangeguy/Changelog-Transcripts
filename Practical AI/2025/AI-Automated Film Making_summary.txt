• The speaker discusses the challenges of applying AI models to financial forecasting, as these models are typically shallow and not suitable for content understanding tasks.
• Two main approaches to learning are mentioned: one learns from past data and makes predictions based on that, while the other creates "digital twins" of real people to simulate audience behavior.
• The digital twin approach has an advantage in capturing outliers and new approaches, as it is closer to human behavior and can adapt quickly to changes.
• Reference data is necessary for training AI models, but this can be a challenge in industries where proprietary or copyrighted content is prevalent.
• Open data sources, such as movie summaries and metadata, are available, but streaming data is often not publicly accessible.
• The speaker discusses the issue of fair value calculations for streaming platforms, which can lead to one-sided relationships between platforms and creators.
• Different models require different types of data: content models need more content data, while financial models look at metadata and financial results.
