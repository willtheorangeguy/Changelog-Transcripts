• Maintainer Lassie Collin claims tarballs were created by Gia Tan, who also signed them
• Gia Tan's identity is unknown, but appears to be a fake or pseudonym
• Analysis suggests that Gia Tan may have been using plausible-sounding Chinese names
• Incident is described as a "nightmare scenario" for supply chain attacks
• The process of an attack on an open-source project is discussed, focusing on "step zero" where the attacker gains trust.
• An email thread shows how the original maintainer's burnout and lack of response led to the attacker inheriting trust.
• The conversation touches on the challenges faced by maintainers in passing on their projects due to the need for specific skills and knowledge.
• A Substack writer proposes that a key issue is the limited activity required for maintaining small, foundational OSS libraries.
• This can make it difficult to build a sustainable community around such projects.
