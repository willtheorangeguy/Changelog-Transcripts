• Brian Chimesky, founder of Zurb, shares his background and how he started the company
• He worked as a toy inventor in the late 90s and learned about presenting ideas and selling concepts without a final product
• When the web became commercial, he applied his skills to building products and services online, focusing on user experience and understanding what drove people to use things
• Zurb started small, as a freelance effort, with Brian working with clients and learning from their problems over five or six years
• The company grew through referrals and only recently began sharing its knowledge and code with the wider audience
• Zurb's mantra is to design for people and focus on helping clients solve problems online
• Zurb's core competency is product design, specifically understanding how to take interactions and design strategy to build better products and services
• The company was founded by a toy inventor who had to figure out problems and present ideas, which has carried over into the company's approach of coming up with new ways of looking at problems
• Zurb introduced Foundation, a framework that allows designers to start building for multiple devices from the beginning
• The team emphasizes iterating, prototyping, and evolving services, rather than just focusing on coding
• Building a strong core foundation was key in creating the current team and company culture
• The company prioritizes hiring people who are a good cultural fit and has implemented a system where new employees must be approved by everyone in the business before being accepted as part of the team
• Close-knit teams are typically small (4-5 people) for effective collaboration
• As team size increases, quality and enjoyment of work suffer
• Incentives include profit sharing, direct employee compensation, and free services like house cleaning
• Social activities like lunches and conversations about personal lives foster a sense of community
• Seeing the impact of one's work on customers is a significant incentive for employees
• Sharing knowledge and resources (e.g. code base, design patterns) has helped the company build relationships with the community and receive reciprocation
• Openness and willingness to learn from others has been key to the company's growth
• Importance of sharing information and knowledge to gain more insights and problem-solving capabilities
• Transition from holding onto proprietary information to making everything open
• Global recognition and unique insight into complex web problems through collaboration with over 200 startups
• Client portfolio including well-known brands such as Logitech, Netflix, and Britney Spears
• Company growth focused on solving bigger and better problems, rather than increasing revenue or hiring more staff
