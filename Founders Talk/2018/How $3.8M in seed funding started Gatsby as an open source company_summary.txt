• Adam Stacoviak interviews Kyle Mathews, CEO and co-founder of Gatsby
• Starting a company around open source software is different from traditional startups
• Kyle's decision to start a company around Gatsby was based on evidence of its commercial value
• This included contract work, sustained usage, and interest from large companies
• The conversation explores the concept of capturing value in open source projects
• Kyle shares his experience with previous attempts at starting a business, including one that failed in the Drupal community
• He reflects on lessons learned from past mistakes and how they informed his decision to build a company around Gatsby
• The challenges of starting a new venture and learning from past mistakes
• The importance of open source in business and Gatsby Inc.'s reliance on dependencies such as Node, Webpack, and React
• Forming good relationships with other parties in the ecosystem to drive success
• The goal of creating a business model that can improve existing tools like Webpack and Babel
• Defining the line between Gatsby the project and Gatsby the company
• Understanding the user experience and developer experience in building sites with Gatsby
• Targeting commercial activity for value capture
• Cloud services as a key area for commercialization
• Limitations of open source in solving complex problems at scale
• Need for commercial solutions to manage large teams and projects
• Importance of combining technical expertise with entrepreneurial mindset
• Unique combination of skills and experiences that enable spotting opportunities
• High bar to meet before entering entrepreneurship due to many people trying obvious ideas

• Gatsby's mission is to improve developer experience and user experience of building websites/apps
• Types of improvements: making workflows better, making things more accessible, reducing bugs, and eliminating the need for a workflow/action altogether
• Disruptive vs sustaining innovation, with Gatsby aiming to disrupt current state of web development
• Current problems in web development that Gatsby aims to solve:
	+ Performance tuning (sites being slow out of the box)
	+ Running websites (scaling, setting up servers, monitoring)
• Automating traffic handling and scaling as a standard feature in website development
• Simplifying development environments through tools like Gatsby

• Key hires include UX researcher, designer, two community contributors, developer relations person, cloud infrastructure engineers, and marketing and sales personnel
• The company prioritized building a strong open source foundation before focusing on commercial products and infrastructure
• Growth metrics show website traffic, downloads, and stars increasing 15-20% per month
