• Ensemble programming, also known as mob programming, is a practice where a team of developers work together on a single problem or task, sharing knowledge and expertise in real-time.
• The ideal ensemble size is typically 4-6 people, with each person contributing their unique perspective and expertise to the problem-solving process.
• The team works together on a single screen, with each person taking turns typing and making decisions, and everyone's knowledge is incorporated into the code.
• The practice is meant to promote collaboration, reduce errors, and increase productivity by sharing the load and leveraging each other's expertise.
• Ensemble programming can be done remotely, but is more effective when done in-person.
• The practice has the property that all decisions are voiced, and everyone has the opportunity to contribute to the problem-solving process.
