• History of Open Data Kit (ODK) started in 2008 as a project at Google among interns Yaw Anokwa, Carl Hartung, and Waylon Brunette
• Project was inspired by a professor's interest in "Ubiquitous Computing" and a talk by Neal Lesh, who helped hospitals with computing problems in Africa
• Initial goal was to build an open source medical records system, but shifted to mobile data collection with the release of Android
• ODK was designed to work on multiple devices and was funded by Andry Rubin, head of the Android team
• Project has had significant impact, with thousands of organizations using ODK, including the Gates Foundation, USAID, and the World Health Organization
• ODK has been used in various projects, including monitoring rain forests, observing elections, and tackling Ebola in West Africa
• Recent example of ODK's use is in the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, where over 1 million submissions have been made using ODK tools
• ODK is widely used in various fields such as healthcare, conservation, climate monitoring, and elections
• The software is used to collect data on paper forms offline, with GPS coordinates and pictures
• ODK has been used in space, on a space station, and in various countries around the world
• The software's open-source nature and being free to use are key factors in its widespread adoption
• Luck and timing played a significant role in ODK's success, coinciding with the rise of Android and increased smartphone and cellular infrastructure in developing countries
• Early goals for ODK included making it easy to try, use, modify, and scale, which were achieved through a focus on user experience and support
• The software's ease of use was critical in its success, particularly for users with limited technical expertise and experience with smartphones.
• The Open Data Kit (ODK) software scales rapidly, requiring ease of use and flexibility to accommodate thousands of users
• Maintaining and evolving the software is a challenge due to its flexibility and widespread use
• User bug reports are rare, and the team relies on logging and betas to gather feedback
• The ODK software has two suites: ODK (common case) and ODKX (complex workflows)
• The core ODK tools include ODK Collect (mobile app), ODK Aggregate (server), ODK Build (form designer), and ODK XLS Form (Excel-based form designer)
• The software allows for offline data collection and submission, with a focus on usability for lightly-trained users and multiple languages
• The team has designed the software to be modular, allowing users to choose the tools that best suit their needs
