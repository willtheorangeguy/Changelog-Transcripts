• JSHint was created as a fork of JSLint to provide a more configurable and less opinionated alternative.
• JSLint was a professional-grade JavaScript linter that was self-hosted and written in JavaScript, but was criticized for being overly opinionated and not allowing developers to configure its rules.
• Anton Kovalyov created JSHint as a more flexible and configurable linter, with the goal of making it easier for developers to use and more approachable.
• The name "JSHint" was chosen to convey the idea that it was a more gentle and less heavy-handed approach to code analysis compared to JSLint.
• The project gained popularity and became widely adopted, with its value proposition being clear and its name being a clever play on the word "lint".
• Mike Pennisi became involved in the project around 2013-2014 and contributed to its development, eventually becoming a maintainer.
• The project's adoption was hindered by a license issue, with the original MIT Expat license being too restrictive and causing concerns among developers.
• The license was eventually changed to a more permissive one, formalized in 2020.
• The MIT license with a clause prohibiting use for "evil" purposes was added to a project, causing controversy and concerns about licensing.
• The clause makes the license unenforceable and unclear, causing difficulties for people who want to use the project.
• The addition of the clause changed the project's licensing from a clear and straightforward MIT license to a more ambiguous one.
• The project's maintainer, Mike Pennisi, was initially unaware of the importance of licensing and only became more aware of it as he worked on the project and learned more about open source and free software.
• The issue of the license was discovered through an issue filed by a contributor on GitHub, and it was eventually removed from the project.
• The problem of the license was not widespread, but rather affected a small number of people who cared about licensing and were deterred from using the project due to the unclear license.
