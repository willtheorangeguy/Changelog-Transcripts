• Introduction to The Changelog podcast and its hosts, Adam Stacoviak and Andrew Thorpe
• Sytse 'Sid' Sijbrandij, co-founder of GitLab, is interviewed about GitLab's features and history
• GitLab is described as a code and project management system that allows for collaboration and continuous delivery of software
• Sid discusses GitLab's history, starting in 2011, and its growth in popularity
• The differences between GitLab and other version control systems, such as GitHub and BitBucket, are touched on
• GitLab's support for Git and its open-source, MIT-licensed nature are mentioned
• The release of GitLab 6.0 is discussed, including its new features, such as the ability to combine groups of people and projects.
• Enterprise edition introduction in 6.0 release
• Enterprise edition only available to subscribers of gitlab.com
• Reception to enterprise edition has been positive, with significant increase in subscribers and major enterprises signing up
• Licensing discussion: GitLab initially considered a commercial license, but ultimately decided to MIT-license the enterprise edition
• Contributing community features to enterprise edition: GitLab will consider merging contributed features if they are found to be useful for smaller organizations, but will prioritize seriousness and quality of code
• Plugin system and abstracting enterprise features: GitLab may package some enterprise features as services or plugins, but will not complicate the codebase if it's not feasible
• Hosting GitLab on GitHub: decision was made for convenience and to focus on the product rather than hosting infrastructure
• GitLab's current limitations with public repositories and plans to address this in the future
• Sid Sijbrandij's pragmatic approach to making decisions based on what users want, not just personal pride
• GitLab's growth and increasing visibility, which may attract attention from GitHub
• GitLab's contributions to other open-source projects, including GitLab CI and GitLab's wrapper around the Grit project
• The reasons behind Grit's stalled development and the impact on GitLab's decision to create its own wrapper around Grit
• GitLab's issue tracker is hard to maintain and requires a team effort
• GitLab Shell is a unique piece of code that allows hosting SSH sessions and cloning/pushing code
• GitLab has a dedicated team of two community members (Ben Bodenmiller and Robert Schilling) who help maintain the issue tracker in their spare time
• GitLab has become sustainable with the introduction of GitLab Enterprise Edition and has hired co-founders to work on the project full-time
• A business model of offering a free open-source product and a paid enterprise edition is becoming more popular, particularly among developers who understand the costs of maintaining open-source projects
• GitLab's business model relies on the success of its users, allowing the company to make money from projects that require the software to function
• Having a commercial offering, such as an enterprise edition, is beneficial as it allows for donations to be disguised as software purchases
• Despite being open-source, GitLab has not faced significant pushback from companies due to its open-source nature
• The company is preparing to hire for sales and support roles, but has not yet encountered issues with companies objecting to the open-source nature of the software
• Sid Sijbrandij notes that finding people who are enthusiastic about inbound sales can be a challenge, but the company is still seeing positive growth
• GitLab is preparing to release version 6.1, which will include features such as issue referencing, API user impersonation, and project-specific IDs
• The company has stated that it does not have a long-term roadmap, preferring to focus on short-term goals and flexibility
• GitLab's approach to not having a long-term plan or roadmap
• The importance of flexibility and being inspired by current needs and opportunities
• The issue with trying to predict and plan for features and products too far in advance
• The idea of having a general direction or vision, but not being tied to a specific roadmap
• The benefits of continuous delivery and releasing features as they are completed
• The need to be adaptable and willing to change course based on community feedback and new ideas
• The challenges and limitations of trying to accurately predict the future and plan for it
